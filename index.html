The problem

Low power factor is a hidden line item on your electricity bill

Power factor is the ratio of working power to total power — the working power plus the non-productive reactive power circulating through your facility. Technically, it's the cosine of the angle between voltage and current.

A power factor between 0.95 and 1.0 means your electrical power is being put to maximum use. Anything below 0.9 means you're drawing more current than you need to deliver the same load — and paying for it through power factor penalties, reactive power charges, or surcharge fees.

Equipment like transformers, motors, air conditioners, furnaces, rolling mills, spinning mills and variable drives all shift the phase angle between current and voltage — which is exactly what drives that penalty up.

Every industrial and commercial facility that runs motors, transformers, air-conditioning systems, furnaces, or variable-speed drives generates what engineers call reactive power — power that circulates through your electrical network without doing any useful work. Reactive power shifts the phase angle between voltage and current, and the ratio of useful (active) power to total (apparent) power is called power factor. When power factor falls below 0.90, state utilities like MSEDCL impose penalties and surcharges on your electricity bill. Improving power factor is therefore not just good engineering — it is directly profitable.

What is an APFC Panel?

An Automatic Power Factor Correction (APFC) panel monitors your facility's power factor in real time and automatically switches capacitor banks in and out in precise steps to keep your power factor as close to unity (1.0) as possible. Unlike fixed capacitor banks — which apply a constant reactive compensation regardless of actual load and can over-compensate, dragging your power factor into the "leading" zone and triggering a different penalty — an APFC panel matches compensation exactly to the instantaneous demand. The result is optimum power factor at all times, eliminating penalties and reducing current draw from the grid.

What Makes Punit Powercare's Thyristorised APFC Panels Different?

Punit Powercare is one of the pioneers in developing and manufacturing Thyristorised APFC panels in the Nashik region — and the difference in technology is significant. A conventional contactor-based APFC panel uses electromechanical contactors to switch capacitor steps. Contactors have moving parts, wear out over time, and most critically, they switch randomly in the AC cycle — generating inrush current spikes that stress capacitors, shorten transformer life, and cause chattering and sparking at the switching contacts.

Punit Powercare's Thyristorised (thyristor-based) APFC panels use solid-state SCR thyristors that fire at the precise zero-crossing point of the AC voltage waveform. This means:

  • Zero inrush current on every capacitor switch — protecting capacitors and transformers.
  • No voltage or current surge — smoother power quality across your entire facility.
  • No chattering, no sparking — solid-state switching at 200V is completely silent and clean.
  • No moving parts — genuinely maintenance-free operation and maximum uptime for the capacitor bank.
  • Small, precise steps — tighter compensation that holds power factor nearer to unity, maximising savings.

The panels also include a live parameter display with RS-232 printout on demand, giving your maintenance team full visibility of the system's performance at any time.
